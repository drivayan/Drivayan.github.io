The Last
Manual M

A DEEP DIVE INTO THE G87 BMW M2 WITH THREE PEDALS.

// THE_CONTEXT

When the G87 BMW M2 first broke cover, the conversation was dominated by its styling—the boxy arches and the horizontal kidney grilles. But behind the aesthetics lies a mechanical soul that is becoming increasingly rare. This isn't just another performance coupe; it's BMW M's final love letter to the manual gearbox before the inevitable shift to full electrification and automatic dominance.

Engine 3.0L S58 Twin-Turbo
Transmission 6-Speed Manual
Peak Power 460 HP
Curb Weight 1725 KG

The Mechanical Dialogue

Living with the manual M2 is a lesson in patience and reward. The 6-speed shifter has that familiar BMW notchiness—rubberized, yet precise. It requires intent. On a cold morning in the city, the first-to-second shift is stubborn, reminding you that there is fluid and metal warming up beneath you. It’s a human experience that the lightning-fast ZF8 automatic simply cannot replicate.

The S58 engine, detuned slightly from the M3/M4, still feels like a bottomless well of torque. However, the manual transmission adds a layer of complexity. You have to work for the power. There is a slight delay as you modulate the clutch, a moment of mechanical tension, followed by a surge that feels more earned than gifted. The weight is noticeable—1725kg is significant for a small coupe—but the short wheelbase and rear-wheel-drive bias keep it agile enough to feel "M" enough.

The Daily Reality

In traffic, the manual M2 is surprisingly docile. The clutch is light, and the auto-rev-matching (which can be turned off in the iDrive 8 settings) makes downshifts effortless for those lazy commutes. The interior is a massive step up from the previous F87 generation, though the curved display can feel a bit overly digital for a car that prides itself on being analog. The carbon fiber buckets are spectacular to look at but a genuine struggle to climb in and out of daily.

Final Verdict
4.7 / 05 _ ANALOG_SOUL

"It is heavy, the styling is polarizing, and it is slower than its automatic sibling. Yet, it is the only M car I would spend my own money on. It demands your attention, and in 2026, that is the ultimate luxury."
